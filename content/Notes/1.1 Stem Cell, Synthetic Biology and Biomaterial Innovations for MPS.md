**Nicholas Kurniawan, Eindhoven University of Technology**
**Probing the dynamics of cell mechanosensing using photoresponsive materials**

Great talk on mechanosensing, addressing the key question of how cells are able to sense curvature and how they respond to it. Showed some ways of biofabricating these surfaces with e.g. the intended groove size to test mechanosensitivity.

Later, I had a chat with him during one of the breaks. 

**Antonio J. Ortega (U Maryland)**
**Engineering vision _in vitro_: Functional light-evoked responses from retinal organoids**  

This was purely focussed on retinal organoids and rather specific. The speaker claims that human retinal organoids do not sufficiently recapitulate biology, but by switching to another animal (some monkey?) that has a slightly different set of cone and rod cells, they were able to engineer retinal organoids that more closely resemble actual biology.

**Marta Sacchi, University Grenoble Alpes, CEA**, 
**Engineering programmable mechanical and electrical microenvironments for brain organoid systems**

This talk focussed on a method to produce "synthetic ECM" that does not have the disadvantages of Matrigel (similar to what Eugi from Victor Peperzak's group is doing). However, the rest of the talk was very much focussed on brain organoids so I'm not sure how general their method is.
